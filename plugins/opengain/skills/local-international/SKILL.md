# Local and International SEO

First determine which branch applies. Do not apply local-business advice to a global online product or hreflang advice to a single-market site.

## Local branch

1. Identify whether the business is storefront, service-area, hybrid, or online-only and which locations are real.
2. Review name, address, phone, hours, service areas, location-page uniqueness, contact paths, and LocalBusiness-derived schema for consistency.
3. Assess location discovery, internal linking, map/contact usability, review presentation, and duplicate or doorway-like location pages.
4. Treat business-profile status, rankings, reviews, and citations as unverified unless a connected tool or user-provided export supplies them.
5. Never invent locations, testimonials, ratings, or local presence.

## International branch

1. Map languages, regions, URL patterns, default/fallback behavior, and whether pages are translated, localized, or duplicated.
2. Validate hreflang syntax, supported language/region codes, self-references, reciprocal links, canonicals, indexability, and `x-default` intent.
3. Check that sitemaps, redirects, navigation, and locale selectors preserve crawlable alternatives and do not force users solely by IP or language detection.
4. Review localized titles, currency/units, legal or commercial differences, and content parity where appropriate.
5. Do not recommend hreflang between pages that are not genuine alternatives.

## Output

State the selected branch and market assumptions. Return systemic issues first, then page-specific findings, evidence, recommended fixes, and validation steps. If both branches apply, keep their findings separate and explain interactions.
