# On-Page Content

Optimize for the user's search task and the page's real purpose. Avoid keyword stuffing, arbitrary word counts, and claims that cannot be substantiated.

## Process

1. Establish the target query or topic, intended audience, conversion goal, and page type.
2. Inspect the rendered page and, when available, its source template and content model.
3. Evaluate whether title, H1, introduction, sections, and calls to action consistently satisfy the likely intent.
4. Review title and description for accuracy, distinctiveness, truncation risk, and useful differentiation. Treat descriptions as click-oriented summaries, not ranking guarantees.
5. Review heading hierarchy, scannability, direct answers, examples, definitions, evidence, author/source transparency, and freshness claims.
6. Inspect internal links for descriptive anchors, useful destinations, contextual placement, and orphan risk.
7. Inspect images for meaningful alt text, decorative handling, dimensions, format, loading behavior, and relevance.
8. Separate content gaps from technical or structured-data problems and route those to the appropriate skill.

If a competitor query and two public URLs are available, use `competitor-ranking` for live ranking evidence rather than inferring why one page ranks from page copy alone.

## Output

Provide evidence-backed findings, a recommended page outline or copy changes where useful, and specific before/after examples. Label inferred search intent and unverified competitor observations. Preserve brand voice, factual accuracy, accessibility, and legal constraints.
